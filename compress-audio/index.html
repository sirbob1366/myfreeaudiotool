How audio compression works

This tool re-encodes your file as MP3 at the bitrate you choose. Lower bitrates throw away more audio detail in exchange for smaller files. The engine is FFmpeg running as WebAssembly inside your browser — the first compression downloads it once (~30 MB), then it's cached.

Which bitrate should I choose?

  • 320 kbps — archive quality music; barely smaller than the original for already-compressed files.
  • 192 kbps — transparent for most listeners; a great default for music.
  • 128 kbps — solid everyday quality at roughly 1 MB per minute.
  • 96 / 64 kbps — ideal for voice memos, podcasts and audiobooks where small size matters most.

Because nothing is uploaded, even a 200 MB WAV file compresses without a single byte leaving your device.
